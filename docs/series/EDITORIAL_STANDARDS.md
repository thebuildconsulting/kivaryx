# Editorial standards — Consulting AND technical education

## Audience promise

This series is worth watching to either learn how to investigate infrastructure or evaluate THE BUILD as a consulting team. Every consequential technology decision must be understandable to engineers without hiding the architecture, mechanisms or costs.

## Narrative formula

1. Present realistic business symptoms, permission/scope and uncertainty.
2. Map current architecture, establish the hypothesis and show evidence.
3. Teach the relevant foundational technology/architecture (not unrelated introductory material).
4. Compare options, identify trade-offs and choose a defensible course.
5. Make a reversible change if justified, test it and present the observed result.
6. Close with the business interpretation, unknowns and next question.

## Length and playlist

There is **no hard 20-minute limit** for Kivaryx. A reasonable target is 25–35 minutes; go longer if it makes the teaching and consulting decision clearer. A difficult topic can be split into multiple related episodes. Episode count is flexible from an initial 12-episode roadmap.

## Truth and provenance

- Within the first 20 seconds: disclose `Fictional fintech simulation — independent lab — synthetic data`.
- Persistent labels when needed: `FICTIONAL CLIENT BRIEF`, `PROPOSED CLOUD ARCHITECTURE`, `LOCAL KIND LAB`, `LAB MEASUREMENT`, `DECISION / ESTIMATE`.
- No real customer transactions, PAN/CVV, employer systems or personal details. No suggestions of a real financial institution's certification.
- Never present intentionally faulty lab configuration as an observed real-company incident.
- No claimed real savings, compliance certification, model accuracy, fraud detection success or availability without appropriate evidence.
- Technologies' current versions and capabilities must be checked against official sources before demonstration.

## Screens / visuals

- Every demo begins with the affected business operation before drilling into components.
- Teach with clear diagrams of control vs data paths, trust boundaries, failure modes and request flow.
- Maintain names and request IDs from episode to episode; diagrams-as-code in `diagrams/`.
- Provide chapters and enough on-screen time to understand concepts, not a montage of terminals.

## Publishable companion assets

Episode technical notes, lab steps, evidence links, architecture map, decision record (ADR), and an executive summary. Prepare 2–3 short clips where they genuinely work. Editor makes first cut; creator validates all technical claims and approves publication.
